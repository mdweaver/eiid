**Recommendation: minor revision.** The authors have answered the objections that justified major revision. I would accept the pooled-model theory after the focused corrections below. The continuous-table identification result, the district covariance decomposition, the global binned outer bound, the optimal-face criterion, and the analytic counterexample to naive CR inference can stand. ## Corrections before acceptance

**1. The equal-rates witness establishes feasibility of the CR union.** PHASE2c line 161 claims it satisfies both weak branches. At this witness the between association is zero. The within association can have either sign, and both branches contain the witness only if it too is zero.

For a three-row example, give equal weight to compositions $(.2,.5,.3),(.5,.2,.3),(.8,.1,.1)$. Set the positive-outcome rate of every group in each unit equal to the first group’s share $X_1$, so $T=X_1$. For group 1 versus the rest, $ER=1$, $\gamma=.24$, and the witness has $D=NM=.24$. Thus $\delta_B=0$ and $\delta_W=.06(1-.24)=.0456$. The positive branch holds. Indeed the negative branch is empty for these margins: it would require both $D\ge1$ and $D\le.24$. This is also a direct check against the corrected M3 applied to the collapsed population.

Suggested text: “The equal-rates table sets every pairwise between association to zero. It therefore satisfies every weak between sign and, for each specified pairing, the CR union. Which branch contains the table depends on its within association.” This preserves the authors’ conclusion about model-free non-refutability. [PHASE2c, lines 156–161](https://github.com/mdweaver/eiid/blob/e170be9161b9bbc250a17a27ffd39bc523c1c88b/docs/extension/PHASE2c_monotone.md#L156-L161)

**2. Make the scope of R10 agree with the response letter.** The document’s opening assigns grouped R3 and R10 to open item R16. The setting repeats the pooled scope. The formal theorem then says “Pooled M0, or M1/M2 in regime R1.” There are two distinct claims here. P3 supplies baseline inference under appropriate fixed-district conditions. This review establishes the sign-augmented procedure for the pooled case under its version-specific assumptions. Following the authors’ stated decision, delete “or M1/M2 in regime R1” from R10 and retain the fixed-district baseline result as background. The change matters because an applied reader will rely on the theorem statement when reporting a grouped confidence interval. [PHASE5a, opening scope](https://github.com/mdweaver/eiid/blob/e170be9161b9bbc250a17a27ffd39bc523c1c88b/docs/extension/PHASE5a_sign_module.md#L3) · [R10, lines 229–255](https://github.com/mdweaver/eiid/blob/e170be9161b9bbc250a17a27ffd39bc523c1c88b/docs/extension/PHASE5a_sign_module.md#L229-L255)

**3. Distinguish an exact rank reduction from a numerical diagnostic.** I accept revised P3.5(b2)(ii-b). It assumes a fixed matrix $L$ and a known affine parameterization in which both $\beta=L\eta+b$ and $\hat\beta=L\hat\eta+b$ hold exactly, with positive definite covariance in the reduced coordinates. The response describes discarding candidates whose gradients use a numerical null direction of the estimated covariance as “case (ii-b).” Discarding candidates can widen an interval. It does not establish these identities or calibrate a rank-based critical value for the retained directions. The exemption for arbitrary candidate collections operates *after* a simultaneous coverage event has been justified.

Claim the projection theorem for full rank or a documented exact reduction, such as eliminating a column using the known adding-up identity. If rank is inferred from a numerical cutoff, describe the guard as a safeguard, or supply a result for the retained subspace and its critical value. This comment does not assert an empirical failure of the guard. [P3.5(b2)(ii), lines 179–193](https://github.com/mdweaver/eiid/blob/e170be9161b9bbc250a17a27ffd39bc523c1c88b/docs/extension/PHASE3_inference.md#L179-L193)

**4. Qualify the premise of N2.3(d).** The definition of N2 requires monotonicity on the finite support. The derivative implication needs the structural functions to be nondecreasing on a neighborhood of the evaluation point. Even a differentiable extension of data that are constant on the support can have a negative derivative at a support point. Take both rows equal to $.5+h(x)$ with $h=(x-1/4)(x-1/2)(x-3/4)$. Both equal $.5$ at all three design points, but both derivatives at $1/2$ equal $-1/16$. The revision already contains this construction for derivative nonidentification. Add “on a neighborhood of $x$” to part (d). The decision to drop the local rows resolves the practical concern. [PHASE2c, support convention](https://github.com/mdweaver/eiid/blob/e170be9161b9bbc250a17a27ffd39bc523c1c88b/docs/extension/PHASE2c_monotone.md#L203-L212) · [N2.3, lines 248–256](https://github.com/mdweaver/eiid/blob/e170be9161b9bbc250a17a27ffd39bc523c1c88b/docs/extension/PHASE2c_monotone.md#L248-L256)

## Disposition of the earlier major comments

| Issue | Second-round assessment |
|---|---|
| Integer versus continuous tables | Resolved. In the unrestricted two-row, two-column problem, the integer first-group positive counts in each unit are consecutive integers, and sums of integer intervals have no holes. This proves the lattice formula. For a nonempty signed integer set, the endpoint loss is less than one lattice step. A nonempty continuous singleton can still miss the lattice at any electorate size. The step is $1/N_1+1/N_0$; an application-specific numerical value needs both group totals. |
| District slopes and pooled signs | Resolved. M5 now uses the full stacked mean map, and total covariance under each group’s weights retains the between-district term. Nonnegative slopes give the within-district result. The pooled sign still requires an assumption about the between-district component. |
| Finite support, size conditioning, and derivatives | Resolved for the global outer LP. The derivative has been withdrawn as an observable on the finite design. Explicitly deferring full-law sharpness is acceptable. On this finite design, there is a short sufficiency proof for N2.2′ when “conditional expectation of a random set” denotes its selection expectation: for each positive-mass $(x,n)$, take a measurable selection with conditional mean equal to the *same* candidate $m(x)$. Glue the selections across the finitely many design atoms. Accounting holds almost surely, and the shape restrictions already hold for the common $m$. This characterization does not imply that a procedure using only $E[T\mid X]$ achieves it. |
| Binning and degree selection | Resolved for the global target. Weight by the number of people in the relevant row within each bin. Ordered, nonoverlapping bins preserve the direction of monotonicity, and recombining with bin row masses preserves the target. The equality uses the model-implied bin mean of $T$; an observed bin mean estimates it when outcomes are random. Prespecifying the degree resolves selection; correct specification or a bias allowance remains necessary. |
| R3 sharpness and refutation | Resolved for pooled M0, subject to correction 1 in the companion text. Remarks R3.1 and R3.2 demonstrate why both the information set and the base model must be named. At line 30, narrow “every computation is a finite LP” to the regression-only optimization over $\Theta_O\cap C$. A polyhedral $C$ need not give a finite representation of the full-law $\Theta_I$. |
| R2 face criterion and redundancy | Resolved. The feasibility problem includes the target intercept. The zero incremental gain of a row is reported for the specified target and stack. Set redundancy has its own inclusion test. |
| R10 union, emptiness and naive interval | Resolved for the restricted scope, with corrections 2–3 above. The true-branch inclusion proves point coverage without splitting the level between branches. The full-candidate CLR assumptions are now explicit and the default simulated-candidate procedure is labelled algorithmic. The fixed-design proof establishes persistent undercoverage of the naive interval. |
| Background repairs | The restricted-measure support corrects the extension from almost-sure equalities on trimmed sets. The Chebyshev/Lindeberg subsequence proof handles a residual variance that vanishes relative to its envelope. |

One correction to my own appendix carries through to the revision. The executable joint-column example was correct; its prose used the wrong sign description. For complementary outcome columns, $\delta_{W,2}=-\delta_{W,1}$. Imposing the **same weak sign** on both columns forces both associations to zero and gives $D=ER$. Opposite signs duplicate one restriction. Please change “opposite within signs” in the description of that example. Also distinguish a branch of latent tables from its singleton *gap image* in M3(d), and the set of slack values preserving a conclusion from its *frontier* in M2(d). ## Checks performed

I reran the complete revised PHASE2c and R10 scripts and the new targeted checks T1c, T4b, S6 and S7. PHASE2c reported **26/26 PASS**. R10 reproduced the analytic limiting upper bound **0.933525** with population critical values and **0.940092** with 4,000 Gaussian draws. In the false-CR experiment at 600 precincts and the largest departure, the CR interval covered **0/400** times and was empty **0/400** times. This illustrates a useful distinction for applications: a restriction can produce a nonempty, precise interval even when it excludes the true gap. The targeted checks confirmed the intercept repair, target-specific redundancy, and both R3 examples. I separately computed the three-row branch counterexample and the derivative at the middle atom above.

The response describes every new check as failing on an earlier assertion. That overstates the evidence. For example, V10 checks the old containment claim, V11 checks interval lengths, and C13 gives a smooth witness for a non-implication that was already true. The numerical checks support those individual conclusions; the theorem proofs determine their generality.
