## Follow-up for Opus: within-precinct parallel trends

**Requested revision.** Give researchers an explicit option to assume parallel trends **in expectation within each precinct**. This is the owner's preferred substantive comparison for the veterans application. Retain electorate-wide parallel trends as a separate choice. The request leaves the software default open. Promote this part of G8 into the main causal theory and the worked example; the original proof verdicts below concern the version at the pinned commit.

### The causal assumption and the comparison it changes

Keep the fixed electorate, a binary vote outcome, no anticipation, and zero mean spillover onto non-veterans in each precinct for this comparison. Write $B_{ig,t}(0)$ for group $g$'s rate in precinct $i$ at election $t$ if nobody served. Condition throughout on the fixed precinct design $\mathcal X$. The new option says that, for every veteran precinct with comparison support,
$$
E[\Delta B_{iV}(0)\mid\mathcal X]=E[\Delta B_{iN}(0)\mid\mathcal X].
$$
Veterans and non-veterans may start at different levels. Each precinct can have its own untreated trend. The expectation is over voting outcomes under the specified regime with the design held fixed. Simply choosing a person at random from a fixed schedule of potential ballots gives a finite-population average and retains the divisibility problem in finding 5. Keep E16's realised-table interpretation available under its own label.

Let $w_{gi}=A_{ig}/A_g$ be the share of group $g$ living in precinct $i$, and $d_{gi}=E[\Delta B_{ig}^{obs}\mid\mathcal X]$. Both options target the expected programme effect on the **same veterans**. For a given set of factual means they impute different untreated changes:

| Assumption | Imputed untreated change for veterans | Implied expected ATT |
|---|---|---|
| Within each precinct | $\sum_i w_{Vi}d_{Ni}$ | $\sum_i w_{Vi}(d_{Vi}-d_{Ni})$ |
| Across the electorate | $\sum_i w_{Ni}d_{Ni}$ | $\sum_i w_{Vi}d_{Vi}-\sum_i w_{Ni}d_{Ni}$ |

For example, in the report's four-precinct design the precinct with 190 veterans and 10 non-veterans supplies **47.5%** of the within-precinct comparison trend and **2.5%** of the electorate-wide comparison trend. Its ten non-veterans stand in for 190 local veterans under the first assumption. Report the share of veterans living in precincts with few or zero comparison voters. If some precincts have no non-veterans, either identify the supported-population target explicitly or retain the full ATT with bounds or a stated extrapolation assumption for the unsupported component. Pooling precincts into composition bands is a third choice of comparison population. FD-LCE can borrow information across compositions while the causal comparison stays within each precinct.

### What to change in M-Count, FD-LCE and inference

1. **M-Count.** Optimise the realised descriptive target $D_W=\sum_i w_{Vi}(\Delta n_{iV}/A_{iV}-\Delta n_{iN}/A_{iN})$ over the existing joint table, using supported precincts and the weights for the stated target population. With known group sizes this is a linear count objective. Keep any restrictions spanning precincts or periods in that same optimisation. In the original four-precinct base-to-post example, margins alone give endpoints of **[−93.87, +144.72] points** for this descriptive target and **[−40.75, +79.25]** for the pooled descriptive target. A difference between two changes can exceed 100 points. Neither interval is a confidence interval for an expected ATT. Re-evaluate any CR or sign row against the new objective before interpreting the earlier positive result.
2. **FD-LCE.** Fit the same two models of expected factual change, $d_V(x)$ and $d_N(x)$, under either PT choice. For the within-precinct objective evaluate both at $X_i$ and give both $w_{Vi}$. In the two-group parameterisation this yields $\sum_i w_{Vi}q^\Delta(X_i)+\Delta\kappa$ under the stated FD mean model (E7–E9). For sharp causal bounds, require $E[B_{iV,0}\mid\mathcal X]+d_{Ni}\in[0,1]$ in **every** supported precinct under within-precinct PT. Pooled PT imposes the analogous rate constraint on the electorate-wide imputation. FD leaves baseline levels free; retain compatible latent baseline means or label the change-only projection an outer bound. Thus the geographic weights differ for each candidate fit, and the feasible fits can differ after imposing the causal rate limits.
3. **Inference.** Under the stated assumptions $ATT^{mean}=E[D_W\mid\mathcal X]$. If M-Count gives $L\le D_W\le U$ under true count rows and an allowance $r_\alpha$ covers $|D_W-E[D_W\mid\mathcal X]|$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$, then $[L-r_\alpha,U+r_\alpha]$ covers the expected ATT at that level. Derive the allowance with the new precinct weights and stated dependence assumptions. Identify belief rows separately from accounting facts. For relative-magnitude sensitivity on expected trends, specify whether the restriction holds within each precinct or after veteran-weighted aggregation, and construct a joint region for the corresponding mean pre-trends. The earlier E25 coverage result for the realised target does not establish coverage for this new target.

### Baseline, post-election and first-difference LCE in the same solver

**Any two imply the third.** For each group $g$, let $m_{g0}(x)$ and $m_{g1}(x)$ be the expected factual yes-vote rates in the two elections, conditional on the same design and indexed by the same precinct composition $x$. Write $d_g(x)=m_{g1}(x)-m_{g0}(x)$. If $m_{g0}(x)=a_g+b_gx$ and $d_g(x)=c_g+e_gx$, then $m_{g1}(x)=(a_g+c_g)+(b_g+e_g)x$. Subtracting the two level functions gives the converse, and subtracting the change from the post-election function gives the baseline. Thus any two exact LCE restrictions give the same feasible set as all three. Slopes may differ by group and election. If each election uses its own changing composition, the difference can depend on both compositions and this equivalence need not hold.

**Comparison to implement.** Use one linear program with baseline means $m_{g0,i}$, changes $d_{gi}$ and post-election means given by their sum. In both fits, retain the restrictions from both elections’ aggregate margins (E8 for the one-pair mean model), the factual rate limits, and the same within-precinct parallel trends assumption in expectation. Under no anticipation and zero local mean spillover, require $0\le m_{V0,i}+d_{Ni}\le1$ and minimise or maximise $\sum_i w_{Vi}(d_{Vi}-d_{Ni})$. First impose LCE only on changes. Then add LCE on each group’s baseline mean, leaving its slope free. The second fit also enforces post-election LCE. Any tightening measures the contribution of baseline linearity under the maintained assumptions. Within-precinct parallel trends requires its own causal justification in both fits.

**Numerical check for Opus.** Take three equally sized precincts with veteran shares of 25%, 50% and 75%. In that order, fix the aggregate yes rates at (0.5%, 20.5%, 40.5%) before and (25.5%, 15.5%, 0.5%) after. Treat these margins as fixed in the synthetic population law and leave the group votes unknown. Holding the causal assumptions above fixed, change LCE gives an expected veteran ATT interval of **[−49.33, +7.33] percentage points**. Adding baseline LCE gives **[+2.67, +7.33]**. As a solver check, imposing pre and post LCE directly should reproduce the second interval, as should imposing all three. These are identification bounds. The example uses no audit or assumed veteran vote gain.

An optional sensitivity analysis keeps exact change LCE and allows each group’s baseline mean to depart from an affine function by at most $\epsilon$ percentage points at each observed composition. Zero recovers the two-election LCE fit. Increasing the allowance weakly widens the feasible set toward the change-only fit. Reporting when the effect interval first includes zero shows how much baseline curvature the sign conclusion can tolerate.

### Group-by-precinct age data and the veteran counterfactual

**What the records add.** Two precincts can both average age 45 and be half veterans. In one, veterans average 55 and non-veterans 35. In the other, both groups average 45. A precinct-wide mean hides this difference. Use age measured for the target electorate at a common baseline. Even exact counts by veteran status and age leave each group's ordinary margins-only vote bounds unchanged when votes can be allocated freely within that group. The new information concerns who is available for comparison and how differences in age mix enter a specified voting-trend model.

**Option 1. Mean-age sensitivity within precincts.** Let $G_i=(\bar A_{Vi}-\bar A_{Ni})/10$ be the observed age gap in decades. As a sensitivity restriction on *group means*, set the untreated veteran trend to $d_{Ni}+\gamma G_i$, maintaining no anticipation and zero mean spillover on non-veterans. One sufficient rationale is that expected untreated changes at each age follow a common linear age profile for both groups, with a precinct-specific intercept and a common slope $\gamma$ per decade. The group-mean restriction can also be imposed directly. It is weaker than asserting that individual age profile everywhere.

In the existing joint LP, keep the same FD-LCE model of factual changes and optimise
$$
\sum_i w_{Vi}(d_{Vi}-d_{Ni}-\gamma G_i),
\qquad 0\le m_{V0,i}+d_{Ni}+\gamma G_i\le1
$$
with the displayed rate limits in each supported precinct and both elections' margin constraints. Since $G_i$ is known, both the objective and constraints remain linear even if $\gamma$ ranges over a specified interval. At $\gamma=0$ this recovers within-precinct mean PT. A hypothesised slope of 0.02 per decade raises the veteran counterfactual trend by 4 percentage points where the gap is 20 years and leaves it unchanged where the gap is zero. The historical records supply the gap. Report the slope as a sensitivity parameter and show where the effect interval first includes zero. Reoptimise at each slope because the local rate limits can change which factual fits are feasible.

**Option 2. Parallel trends within precinct and baseline age band.** With known counts in common age bands $b$, assume equal expected untreated changes across groups within each supported $(i,b)$ cell. Write $p_{Vib}$ for the share of precinct $i$'s veterans in band $b$. The imputed untreated veteran trend in that precinct is $\sum_b p_{Vib}d_{Nib}$. If 70% of veterans but 20% of non-veterans are older, the older non-veteran trend receives 70% weight. The age-band counts supply these weights and reveal empty comparison cells. Age-specific voting changes remain latent. Introduce group-by-age vote cells that sum to the observed returns, retain feasible counterfactual rates at the band level, and project the same veteran ATT jointly. Report the veteran share lacking local age-band support and bound its contribution if targeting all veterans. Age-band PT and unadjusted precinct PT need not imply one another when age mixes differ, so their intervals need not be nested. A few group-specific quantiles generally do not determine common-band counts.

**Separate use in FD-LCE.** A group’s baseline age summary can also enter its model of *factual* voting change. The precinct mixture then weights each group's predicted change by its population share. Variation in the two age profiles across precincts can help distinguish the group changes under that specification. Assess design rank and compare bounds empirically, since estimating additional coefficients can also widen the set. For the first comparison above, keep factual FD-LCE fixed so that changing the causal age adjustment has a clear interpretation.

## Findings requiring revision

### 1. Later RM horizons accumulate violations (E18(b), lines 367–375). Error.

The shared-branch construction in E18(a) is correct for every post-period. The endpoint-wise containment and its proof in (b) use the first-post bound $|b_t|\le\bar M A(n)$. At horizon $h$, sum the $h$ consecutive differences from $b_0=0$ to obtain $|b_h|\le h\bar M A(n)$.

For a directly observed example, take two pure precincts. The comparison rate is .50 throughout. At elections $-1,0,1,2$ the treated group's realised rates are $(.35,.50,.50,.50)$ and its no-treatment rates are $(.35,.50,.65,.80)$. There is no anticipation or spillover. The largest pre-period change in the gap is .15. Both post increments of the untreated gap equal .15, satisfying RM(1). Yet $D_2=0$ and $ATT_2=-.30$, outside the proposed endpoint-wise range $[-.15,.15]$. The rate limits are slack.

**Repair.** Restrict E18(b) as written to the first post-election and zero spillover, or use $D_h(F)\oplus[-h\bar M\sup_F A-\Lambda,\ h\bar M\sup_F A+\Lambda]$ as a marginal outer set. For joint inference or a contrast across horizons retain the path constraints. Count up to $2L K_F$ scalar branch projections for RM, and $K_F$ for smoothness when the count set itself has $K_F$ branches. The vector projection is a union of polytopes. The paper's definitions of consecutive differences and second differences agree with Rambachan and Roth, eqs. (7)–(8). Allowing interference here gives $b$ and $S$ separate roles, with total bias $b-S$. [Rambachan and Roth (2023), §2.4](https://jonathandroth.github.io/assets/files/HonestParallelTrends_Main.pdf).

### 2. The finite-sample FD regression needs its own uncertainty allowance (E23(b), lines 443–450). Error as a direct invocation of P3.8.

For an estimated linear functional, write the contribution from precinct $i$ as $w_i^\top(T_{it}-T_{iu})$, including zero weight for the omitted category. Its range has length **$2(\max_c w_{ic}-\min_c w_{ic})$**. P3.8 uses the range of a single election. E12 fixes the separate discrepancy between actual and model-implied targets. Adding that discrepancy cannot establish the required regression event.

For instance, use three distinct compositions with 20 independent precincts at each and a saturated quadratic fit. At each precinct a fair coin chooses between all voters moving from no to yes and all moving from yes to no. FD holds with mean zero. The fitted change at the middle composition is the average of 20 independent signed ones. With .025 for each tail, the unmodified level radius is .3037 and covers zero with exact probability **.884682**. Doubling the radius gives **.997423**. A 100,000-replication simulation gives .88431 and .99747. These probabilities concern the regression functional used to build the intervals. They do not estimate coverage of every full ATT interval.

**Repair.** Double the range in each FD regression row and objective allowance of P3.8; apply its error allocation to the rows and objective sides used. Add E12's actual-target allowance separately where required. The prior review identified the range change for a deleted finite-sample claim in E25; E23 still cites P3.8 directly.

### 3. Full rank of the regressors does not ensure the regression CLT or a nonsingular stacked covariance (E23(a)–(c), E25 H1). Gap.

Even with three equally frequent compositions and individually nonsingular election regressions, all elections can have the same random precinct outcome. The stacked coefficient vectors can then be identical. Nonoverlapping changes can also coincide exactly through dependence within precincts. Conversely, overlapping pairs alone do not create an exact linear identity; a redundant contrast, such as including the third edge of a triangle, does. Designs of full rank at each sample size can also have asymptotically negligible information in a direction.

**Repair.** State the design-sequence, leverage or Lindeberg, and covariance-consistency conditions used for the fixed-dimensional OLS CLT. One sufficient design condition with bounded regressors is convergence of the normalised Gram matrix to a positive-definite matrix, with suitable convergence of the variance of the scores. Require positive definiteness of the relevant limiting covariance for the Wald projection, or the exact reduction already specified in H1. Account for cross-election covariance by precinct. A fixed parameter dimension alone does not give validity “in any regime.” Under E23(c) require a correctly specified level mean conditional on the design used for inference and its joint covariance with the change regression. A finite set of evaluated compositions gives a finite LP. Imposing rows only on a sample grid from continuous support is an outer relaxation unless their sufficiency for the full support is established. The open status for inference on E8 is appropriate.

**What survives in E25.** On the joint regression event and all actual-minus-mean residual events, the true ATT path is feasible in at least one branch; weak duality and the union bound give the advertised simultaneous outer coverage with a common error budget. An empty *plug-in* branch cannot be discarded without the inferential construction. Nor can “simultaneously for all branches” mean that every branch interval covers the truth. The simultaneous assertion concerns the reported union across horizons.

**Zero-residual clause.** To interpret the zero-box construction causally for model means, separately define regime-specific means and impose the causal restriction at that level. For example, suppose the pre-DiD equals $+.10$ or $-.10$ with equal probability, the observed post-DiD is zero, $b_1=.10$ and $S=0$. This can be realised with base and observed post rates .50 in both groups, a treated pre rate of .40 or .60, comparison .50, and untreated treated-group post rate .60. RM(1) holds at every realisation but applying RM to the mean pre-DiD forces zero bias and gives zero effect. The mean programme effect is $-.10$. Projection coverage for a feasible model-mean parameter requires that parameter to satisfy the model-mean restrictions. With only FD, the paper's change-only outer procedure is valid under its conditions. Observed count-layer level bounds could still be imposed through an appropriate joint construction.

### 4. A feasible integer counterfactual requires integer comparison counts as well (E16(b), lines 328–340; Remark 7.1). Error for a full finite-person witness.

Consider one treated and one comparison person. Both vote yes after treatment; set $\theta=0$ and $S=1/2$ with an allowance of at least $1/2$. This satisfies MTR, has integer realised counts and integer $A_r\theta$, and satisfies both continuous rate limits. The comparison person would have to contribute half a yes vote under no treatment. The proof correctly constructs integer treated outcomes under its divisibility condition. It constructs only comparison *mass* under the stated comparison condition.

**Repair.** Require $(B_{sct}-S_t)A_s\in\mathbb Z$ as well as $A_r\theta_t\in\mathbb Z$ at every post-period, and use a greedy allocation for each group. Keep the indicator of one designated outcome explicit. For multiple categories, a construction must respect adding-up across categories. Under interference, write $Y(\mathbf z^{obs})$ for the observed regime in the person-level MTR inequality. The estimand is the total effect of the programme assignment on its treated group. Setting $S=0$ assumes zero net effect on the comparison group’s rate. Individual spillover effects could cancel.

### 5. The integer ATT can be a full unit inside the continuous endpoint, with margins alone (Remark 7.1, line 502). Negative result.

Take one precinct, $m$ treated people, $m+1$ comparison people, and $m$ yes ballots at each of two elections. Assume exact parallel trends, no anticipation, and zero spillover. Write $y_0,y_1$ for treated yes counts. Parallel trends implies
$$
\theta=\frac{y_0}{m}-\frac{y_1-y_0}{m+1}.
$$
An integer treated counterfactual requires $(m+1)\mid m(y_1-y_0)$. The denominators are coprime and $|y_1-y_0|\le m$, so $y_1=y_0$. The integer ATT set is **$\{0\}$**. In the continuous problem choose $y_1=m$, $y_0=m^2/(2m+1)$ to get $ATT=1$, or $y_1=0$, $y_0=m(m+1)/(2m+1)$ to get $ATT=-1$. Convexity fills **$[-1,1]$**. Both legal continuous endpoints are therefore one full rate unit, or $m$ lattice steps, from the nearest admissible integer effect. Enumeration and LP agree for $m=2,3,5,10,25,100$.

**Repair.** Delete any suggestion of a universal one-step endpoint guarantee for the margins-only trend dial. The counterexample uses its allowed setting $M=0$. Report the target lattice as a necessary condition and solve the integer problem when individual counts matter. In an applied study, distinguish exact equality of *finite-population realised counterfactual trends* from equality in expectation; the latter would require another theorem and has different arithmetic implications.

### 6. A three-edge star has an explicit integrality gap (E27, lines 492–502). Open extension resolved negatively.

E27's total-unimodularity proof for paths works. Moreover every simple cycle can be oriented cyclically so the proof's two contributions at each vertex have opposite signs, irrespective of chronological edge orientation. This covers maximum-degree-two graphs with cycle components under the same one-column and per-edge laminar assumptions.

For a star, take three precincts with one person per group, one yes ballot per precinct at the base, and zero yes at each of three post elections. Let $x_i\in[0,1]$ be base treated yes counts. Put one change row on each base–post pair, requiring a change of at least $-1$ on its precinct subset. Use subsets $\{1,2\},\{2,3\},\{1,3\}$ on the three different edges. Each edge has a laminar family of one. The rows reduce to $x_1+x_2\le1$, $x_2+x_3\le1$, $x_1+x_3\le1$. The maximum total treated yes count is $3/2$ continuously and $1$ with whole ballots. Both are attained.

**Repair.** Keep E27 and the lattice theorem E26. Record the degree-three class as admitting a strict LP–integer gap, even for integer margins and row bounds with both problems feasible. This resolves more than failure of the TU proof.

### 7. The electorate's pairwise DiD and the same-composition ordering have different loadings (E7(d), lines 165–176). Error.

The exact target loading in E7(b) is $\sum_i w_{ir}(KX_i)_r-\sum_i w_{is}(KX_i)_s$. P-R studies $[(e_r-e_s)^\top K]x$ with **one common composition**. Take $R=3$, $K_{13}=K_{23}=.1$, $K_{12}=0$, with skew symmetry. The first two rows agree, so every common-composition contrast for pair $(1,2)$ is zero. Use equal-size units at the three simplex vertices and three edge midpoints, repeating the $(1,3)$ midpoint once. The quadratic design has full rank. The group-weighted mean of $X_3$ is .20 for group 1 and .125 for group 2. The electorate DiD loading is therefore **.0075**. Baseline binary group rates of .5 with changes $KX$ keep both elections feasible and leave aggregate change zero.

**Repair.** Retain (a)–(c); replace the first sentence of (d) by the weighted expression. Attribute P-R's directional conclusion to the *pair restrictions across compositions*. There is a short general proof of the connected-tree rank statement under full-dimensional composition support: if all edge contrasts of $Kx$ vanish across that support, the two row vectors coincide on each edge (the compositions span $\mathbb R^R$). Connectivity makes all rows identical; their diagonal entries then force that common row to be zero. The joint map is injective. Two-sided bounded contrasts on a spanning collection of compositions consequently bound all coordinates. Whether they improve a particular target depends on the data.

### 8. Pairwise sharp FD sets do not stack freely across shared elections (E8, §§3.2 and 6). Scope gap.

E8 states a correct **one-pair** theorem. Three elections with $T_{it}=.5$ in every unit suffice to distinguish it from a joint path result. Use compositions $x=.4,.5,.6$, so the quadratic design has full rank. For both successive change pairs set $\Delta Q=0$ and $\Delta\kappa=1.25$. Each individual pair admits rates with group changes $(1.25(1-x),-1.25x)$, at all three compositions. At $x=.5$ the treated rate would have to rise by .625 twice. There is no feasible three-election path. Neither the fixed-population stacking lemma nor the product result for per-election level models licences the product of the two E8 sets.

**Repair.** Label pair-by-pair constraints an outer relaxation wherever used for a path. For joint sharp FD, apply the existing Lemma G′ once to the product of *levels*, using an incidence matrix taking all required differences. In support-function language, sum $h_{S_t}((A^\top u)_t)$ over periods before taking the conditional expectation. In a finite-support LP use one latent level per election per support point, shared by every difference. Conservative E25 coverage can use a larger set if so labelled. In E8(c) “strict inclusion is the rule” is numerical experience; a pointwise Jensen gap need not remove a globally feasible parameter if another composition already binds.

### 9. Continuous causal sharpness, refutation, and causal labelling (E14–E17 and E21–E22). Clarifications and one quantifier fix.

* **E16(d).** For several post-periods exact parallel trends and zero spillover are feasible iff **one** table gives $0\le\theta^{PT}_t(n)\le1$ for **every** post-period, absent further causal rows. Consider a known base gap .5. A count row gives comparison post rates $s_1+s_2=1.2$, each in $[.2,1]$. Each period alone can have $s_t\le.5$; the two cannot do so jointly. The tables are feasible with equal group counts, half the electorate voting yes at each election, and base rates .75 and .25. The argument in the text is exact for a single post-period.
* **E15.** For nonempty $F$, the continuous interval $[B^L-1,B^U]$ survives even if $F$ is disconnected: every interval $[B-1,B]$ contains zero. The proof need not assume the realised rate's range is an interval. Pre-period tables can affect $B^L,B^U$ via count rows linking elections. The correct interpretation is that untreated trends add no restriction here and the sign cannot be identified unconditionally. The observed post rate can still limit the effect substantially. Definition 5.1 is a reporting convention. MTR and ND without PS require no spillover condition on an unused comparison group. In §9, explain no anticipation as “treatment did not affect votes before it began.” Voters may change freely between pre-elections.
* **E17.** The repaired equality criterion works for the stipulated one-post dial. Its breakdown formula is for the Minkowski outer bound. If a user requests breakdown of the *sharp* positive conclusion, minimise $M$ subject to joint feasibility and $ATT\le0$, treating refutation separately and recording infinity when no such witness exists.
* **E21.** The continuous endpoint formula is correct for a single designated post-period. Replace the proof's $S=\Lambda$ by $S=\min(\Lambda,B_s)$ at the optimal post table. This respects comparison rate feasibility and leaves the result unchanged since $B_{r0}\ge0$. The max–min LP uses the hypograph of the minimum, one solve per branch of a disjunctive $F$. For simultaneous multi-post restrictions require a shared feasible table.
* **E22.** The pre-period nonidentification witness is sound when both counterfactual rates are feasible at both spillover values. Post-period rate constraints and added causal rows can still restrict $S$. A composition slope refers to an average effect on a binary indicator within a unit. A person-level binary outcome difference only takes values $-1,0,1$.

### 10. When relative magnitudes help (E19 and §9.3). Error in the applied conclusion; boundary fixes.

The lower bound $\bar M w_{-1}/2$ on the worst admissible allowance at fixed base is correct. It does not imply that researchers must add a pre-period model or a belief before RM can help. Take two precincts with group counts $(99,1)$ and $(1,99)$, 50 yes votes in each at both pre-elections, and 70 and 50 yes votes at the post-election. No precinct is pure and the pre-trend is set-valued. With the counts alone, no anticipation, zero spillover and RM(1), the sharp ATT interval is **$[.12,.28]$**. For comparison, the no-trend-assumption interval is $[-.31,.71]$ (treated post yes totals range from 69 to 71 out of 100). Additional restrictions on pre-period voting are unnecessary for a sign conclusion here.

**Repair.** Teach the comparison between the admissible pre-trend magnitude and the post-period signal. E19(c1) establishes *weak* nesting for added deterministic count restrictions. A model-based pre-trend interval instead requires a simultaneous actual-target coverage argument. In (a) the common-maximiser “iff” requires $\bar M>0$; when $\bar M=0$ equality always holds regardless of the maximisers of $A$. In (c3) either specify $\Lambda=0$ or retain $+S$ and the comparison feasibility constraint. In (d) plug-in intervals *can* under-cover. E25 provides a counterexample, not undercoverage in every design.

### 11. Uncertain margins and changing populations (G1–G9 and R32). Gap in coverage and optimisation scope.

In G7(a) the event $a^\circ\in\mathcal V$ ensures coverage only when $n^\circ\in F(a^\circ)$ also holds. With false belief rows, even a singleton containing the true margins with probability one can yield an empty set. Include row and causal assumptions explicitly for G7(a)–(b), or subtract their failure probabilities as in E13. Write the functional as $\varphi_a$ when weights and denominators vary with $a$. Even with a polyhedral margin set the outer union is generally not a single LP. The G7(c) trace correction and injectivity test are sound under the stated conditional mean and matching assumptions. Interpreting DiD-S on a hull of *true* compositions additionally requires the domain to be known, externally specified, or covered along with the weights. The corrected regression identifies $\Delta Q$ under rank; it does not identify every input to an election-level bound.

### 12. Verification and small statement repairs

* E3(c)'s common-base argument is valid. For **consecutive** differences the relevant positive width is that of a *shared interior election*. With three elections numbered 0, 1, 2, only election 0 uncertain and the other two point-identified, the consecutive-difference set **is** its coordinate box. The base-relative vector still has the non-box property. E6 requires both groups to have positive aggregate counts in each bin. A zero-weight cell can be omitted in E4.
* Distinguish attaining both extreme endpoints from filling an entire reported interval. The author checker's `union_range` takes the smallest lower and largest upper bound. That is sufficient for endpoint checks; a general disjunctive set can have holes. Preserve branch intervals, sort and merge overlaps before making a whole-set equality claim. For N21 and the four-precinct RM calculations the separate check supplied here performs that merge and confirms a single interval. In E19(b) the stated sufficient condition really does give the whole no-assumption set because it permits every $\theta$ at every table.
* A sharp RM set can have a hole **even when the count set is one polytope**. In one precinct take three equal-sized groups. Their early, base and post yes rates are respectively $(.75-x/2,.25+x/2,.50)$, $(.75,.25,.50)$ and $(.50+2x,.60,.40-2x)$ for $-.2\le x\le.2$. Every vector sums to 1.5, so the same observable margins and linear count restrictions generate this convex family. Treat group 1 and compare with group 2. The largest pre-change magnitude is $|x|$, and $\theta^{PT}=1.10$. RM(1) and zero spillover require $1.10-|x|\le\theta\le1$. Consequently $|x|\ge.10$, and the two ATT projections are **$[-.90,-.70]\cup[-.30,0]$**. Returning their endpoint envelope would incorrectly include an effect of $-.50$. This is a direct regression test for the future branch implementation.
* E24's union argument works if a fixed true branch has the stated coverage, without a branch multiplicity penalty. For a branch index depending on **realised random tables**, separately valid intervals under fixed-branch models alone need not transfer. Use the common event in E25, or prove coverage conditional on the realised branch selection with an appropriate partition. Do not interpret this result as authorising a data-selected single-branch interval.
* E12's independent-precinct allowance is sound. Its normal bound uses the 6a/P3 variance-subsequence argument; a scale based on ranges alone need not give a CLT standardised by the actual variance. Opposite coefficients on a given pair suffice for FD. For linear combinations of several modelled changes, define the estimand on their span and combine coefficients period by period. Nonlinear functions like the largest absolute pre-trend need a joint region for the constituent linear quantities. E13's identity-row and belief-row coverage distinction should carry through §9.
* In E9 the equivalence of full-law sharpness and inclusion holds for the **parameter** set, and for the monotone scalar target in the two-group case. Preserve the weaker, sufficient-only condition for target projections with more groups in the register. E10's midpoint identity applies to swaps in the specified pair direction. If extending from their midpoints to the whole hull, require that these midpoints are dense there, as on a full-dimensional composition hull, and use continuity.
